San Diego Vacant Homes Tax Fails at Polls; City Faces $146M Budget Deficit +

San Diego voters rejected Measure A (58% opposed), which would have taxed second-home owners $8k-$10k annually and generated $9-21M for the city. The failure leaves the city searching for alternative revenue sources to address a $146M budget deficit next fiscal year, with about 5,100 homes citywide affected by the measure.

Why it matters: Direct impact on San Diego's fiscal health and housing/development policy—budget constraints will likely shape future zoning decisions, affordable housing mandates, and development incentives you'll encounter in the market.

SCAG (Southern California Association of Governments) released the preliminary map implementing SB 79, California's landmark zoning reform that allows multi-unit residential development in previously single-family zones. Why it matters: SB 79 fundamentally reshapes the development opportunity set in CA real estate—understanding which submarkets/jurisdictions are affected by these zoning changes will be essential for multifamily and mixed-use acquisition/development strategies.

Multifamily Starts Plunge 42% as Financing Pressures Mount +

U.S. multifamily housing starts collapsed 41.6% in May to 284,000 units, the steepest monthly pullback in recent memory, as developers face a broken financing equation despite solid underlying demand. The plunge reflects developers' inability to pencil deals in today's rate environment, while single-family starts held relatively steady, signaling this is a financing/development cost story, not demand weakness. With the Fed holding rates steady while signaling hawkish intent (9 officials now expect at least one rate hike in 2026), relief for multifamily developers appears unlikely near-term.

Why it matters: This is a critical market signal—a 42% month-over-month drop indicates cap rate/construction cost misalignment is now forcing deal cancellations, directly impacting your acquisition and development strategy assumptions.

Kevin Warsh's New Fed Strategy Could Redefine CRE Capital Markets +

Fed Chair Kevin Warsh held rates at 3.50%-3.75% in his first meeting but signaled a more hawkish stance, with 9 of 18 policymakers now projecting at least one rate hike in 2026 and inflation forecasts showing 3.6% PCE and 3.3% core PCE. Warsh unveiled a 'new chapter' emphasizing reduced forward guidance and five task forces to review Fed communications, signaling a shift away from market transparency that has defined Fed policy for two decades. For CRE, this means navigating elevated borrowing costs through 2026 with less predictability from the central bank.

Why it matters: Less forward guidance increases market volatility and makes cap rate/refinance modeling harder—you need to stress test deals assuming higher-for-longer rates and potential surprise moves from the Fed.

Hackman Capital Faces $258M Default +

Major industrial developer Hackman Capital is facing a $258M default, signaling distressed asset dynamics emerging in the trophy industrial space as refinancing pressures mount in the current rate environment. This is a high-profile case in Southern California's competitive industrial market, potentially triggering portfolio liquidations or lender-driven asset sales. The default underscores how even well-capitalized developers are struggling with floating-rate debt and aggressive basis assumptions from the 2019-2021 cycle.

Why it matters: This is a live case study in how rate-environment mismatches are forcing distressed sales in premium SoCal industrial assets—watch for the resolution process and potential acquisition opportunities.

All Eyes on the Strait of Hormuz After Iran and US Agree to 60-Day Ceasefire +

The U.S. and Iran signed a 60-day ceasefire framework that includes reopening the Strait of Hormuz, lifting U.S. sanctions, resuming Iranian oil exports, and a $300B U.S.-backed reconstruction plan. Oil prices fell to $73 WTI and $77 Brent following the deal, reversing months of Iran war-driven supply constraints that had taken 12M barrels/day offline. The U.S. Strategic Petroleum Reserve fell to 340M barrels (lowest since 1983) during the supply crisis, signaling how geopolitical risk has impacted energy markets.

Why it matters: Sustained lower oil/energy prices reduce input cost pressure on construction and operating expenses, improving CRE development economics; however, the deal's fragility means rates could spike again if negotiations fail.
